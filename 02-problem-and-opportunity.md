# Part II: Problem & Opportunity

## Market Problem

FreClean's own published materials frame the problem it addresses broadly: reliable, affordable, and environmentally responsible cleaning is not consistently available or accessible across the markets it targets, and economic opportunity in the cleaning sector, whether as an entrepreneur or a small distributor and not only as a customer, is under-developed. **(Confirmed** as FreClean's stated framing; **Assumption** that this framing accurately reflects the addressable market, which this book has not independently validated with primary research.)

## Customer Pain Points

FreClean has not published customer research, survey data, or documented pain-point analysis. **(Not Publicly Disclosed.)** Based on the target markets FreClean has named (see Part V), the following pain points are **reasonable, industry-typical assumptions** for each segment: not FreClean-specific findings:

| Segment | Likely pain point (Assumption) |
|---|---|
| Residential households | Inconsistent quality/reliability of informal cleaning labor; limited access to trusted, professional-grade products |
| Airbnb/property operators | Turnaround-time pressure between guest stays; need for consistent, bookable service |
| Hotels & commercial | Need for contracted, auditable service quality at scale |
| Entrepreneurs/distributors | Limited access to a credible, branded product line to build a business around |

## Market Opportunity

FreClean is pursuing an opportunity with two structurally different halves: a **large, well-established global cleaning products and services market** (see Part VIII for sizing), and a **specific, under-served Haitian and Caribbean market** with real macroeconomic headwinds and real diaspora-driven capital flows that most global cleaning brands do not address directly. This combination is the core of the opportunity thesis, examined fully in Part XIX, *Investment Thesis*.

## Industry Context

Global demand for household and commercial cleaning products has grown consistently in recent years, driven by rising hygiene awareness, urbanization, and demand for eco-friendly formulations, according to multiple independent market-research firms (see [Sources and References](../../sources/REFERENCES.md) and Part VIII for the full range of estimates and their citations). **(Confirmed** as an industry-wide trend, third-party sourced; not FreClean-specific data.)

## Consumer Trends

Independent research cited in Part VIII points to three consistent trends across the cleaning products industry: rising demand for eco-friendly/non-toxic formulations, growth in e-commerce and direct-to-consumer distribution, and continued growth in disinfectant/sanitization demand following the COVID-19 pandemic. **(Confirmed, third-party sourced; see References.)** Whether FreClean's current product roadmap and packaging plans (see Part III) are positioned to capture these specific trends is **TBD**, since packaging, formulation, and e-commerce infrastructure are all still in early or planning stages (see Parts III, X, and XI).

## Business Trends

Two structural trends are relevant to FreClean's specific positioning, both third-party sourced:

- Haiti's diaspora remitted approximately USD 3.8–4.1 billion in 2023–2024, equivalent to roughly 20% of Haiti's GDP: a very large capital flow relative to the size of the domestic economy. **(Confirmed, third-party sourced; see References.)**
- Haiti's domestic economy has contracted for seven consecutive years as of 2025, with inflation averaging 28.3% and an estimated 49% of the population living on less than US$3.00/day. **(Confirmed, third-party sourced; see References.)**

Read together, these two facts describe a market where domestic purchasing power is under real pressure, but where diaspora-linked capital and remittance-funded spending represent a meaningful, distinct customer and investment pool. FreClean's stated interest in digital and Web3 payments (Part XI) may be strategically relevant to this diaspora dimension specifically, connecting Haitian communities abroad to spending or investment in Haiti through modern payment rails, though FreClean has not published any strategy document that makes this connection explicit. **(Assumption,** clearly flagged as this book's own inference, not a FreClean claim.)
