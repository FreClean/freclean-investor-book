# Business Model

*Standalone excerpt of Part VI of the full book. See [`book/chapters/06-business-model.md`](book/chapters/06-business-model.md) for the version with the full Business Model Canvas diagram.*

## Value Proposition

For customers: reliable, affordable, environmentally responsible cleaning from a locally rooted brand. For entrepreneurs and distributors: a route into the cleaning business via training, wholesale access, and franchise support. For the broader ecosystem: a Web3-enabled payment option (USDm/CELO) uncommon in this category.

## Revenue Streams

Professional Cleaning Services (residential, commercial, industrial), Product Manufacturing, Wholesale Distribution, Retail Sales, White-Label Manufacturing, Private Label Production, Franchise Opportunities, Business Consulting, Corporate Partnerships, Training Programs. **(Confirmed** as stated; **TBD** for relative revenue contribution of each.)

## Cost Structure

Direct labor, manufacturing/materials, packaging, distribution/logistics, brand/marketing, and technology/platform costs are the structural categories expected: no specific figures have been published for any of them. **(TBD.)**

## Distribution Model

Direct sales, retail, wholesale, and planned online/e-commerce channels. **(Confirmed** as stated intentions; **TBD** for which are currently active.)

## Customer Acquisition & Retention

No acquisition-cost or retention data exists. **(TBD.)** The most plausible channels are local word-of-mouth/referral and the CeloHT relationship, structurally. **(Assumption.)**

## Partnerships

Open invitation to businesses, hotels, restaurants, retail stores, government agencies, NGOs, educational institutions, environmental organizations, community associations, international organizations, technology companies, and distribution partners. **(Confirmed** as an open invitation; **Not Yet Verified** for any specific named partnership beyond the stated CeloHT relationship.)

## Scalability

Services scale with labor and local operational capacity (harder to scale rapidly). Products scale with manufacturing capacity and distribution reach (currently unconfirmed). Franchise, if executed, is the most naturally scalable revenue line. **(This book's own analytical assessment.)**
